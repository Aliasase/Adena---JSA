## Recognition and Applicability

Accept findings S-1 through S-7 in principle, subject to the following:

- Establish recognizable cues for applicable activities, objects, work means and conditions.
- Infer reasonably foreseeable hazards without requiring crews to explicitly describe the hazardous condition.
- Distinguish initial erection, fit-up and temporary connections from inspection or tightening of existing permanent connections.
- Do not infer suspended-load or temporary erection hazards solely because an existing structural connection is mentioned.
- Do not make control-level applicability dependent exclusively on exact keyword matches.
- Snug-tight, turn-of-nut, pretensioning, tension-control installation and controlled torque application must retain their distinct technical meanings.
- Pretensioning does not necessarily establish that conventional controlled torque application is being performed.

## Regulatory Interpretations

Connector classification depends on the employee's actual duties involving hoisting equipment, placement and connection of structural members under 1926.751.

Final bolting or torquing of previously erected members ordinarily falls under the general steel erection provisions of 1926.760(a), rather than connector-specific provisions under 1926.760(b).

Do not introduce less protective federal exceptions or thresholds into user-facing suggestions where verified Adena or project requirements are more stringent.

1926.302(b)(2) addresses attachment retention on pneumatic impact (percussion) tools. Universal applicability to rotary impact wrench designs has not been established.

Retain socket retention instructions based on manufacturer requirements and safe work practices. Do not require a retaining pin and ring where another approved retention mechanism is used.
