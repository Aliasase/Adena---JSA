Architect Supplemental Directive — Temporary Fall Protection Anchorage Connectors
Incorporate the following into v1.4.1-dev3 alongside the previously authorized corrections.
1. Terminology and Recognition
Recognize common field terminology, including:

* Beamer / Beamers
* Beam strap anchor
* Sliding beam anchor
* Sliding beam clamp
* Sliding beam clamp anchor
* Pivot D-ring beam anchor
* Fall protection beam clamp
* Beam glide anchor
* Temporary beam anchorage connector

Recognize these as temporary fall protection anchorage connectors where terminology or context establishes that application.
Do not confuse these devices with lifting clamps, material-handling trolleys or structural fastening hardware.
Recognize installation, inspection, use, repositioning and removal as distinct activities with appropriate hazard and control relationships.
2. Hazard Relationships
Establish the following applicable exposures:

* Failure or disengagement of a temporary fall protection anchorage connector.
* Sliding beam anchor traveling beyond the end of a beam or through a discontinuity in the supporting flange.
* Improper engagement of the anchor jaws with the beam flange.
* Incompatible beam flange dimensions or configuration.
* Unintentional release or loosening of the anchorage connector.
* Pinch points during installation, adjustment and removal.
* Swing fall exposure.
* Insufficient fall clearance.
* Falls during installation, repositioning or removal of the anchorage connector.
* Dropped anchorage equipment when working at elevation.

Reuse existing canonical hazards where equivalent. Establish a distinct canonical record for unintended travel and disengagement where existing records do not adequately describe that exposure.
3. Required Control Relationships
Develop canonical controls addressing the following:

* Use an approved fall protection anchorage connector compatible with the beam dimensions, configuration and intended application.
* Verify both anchor jaws are properly engaged and locking mechanisms are secured according to manufacturer instructions.
* Inspect the entire intended travel path before using a sliding beam anchor.
* Identify beam ends, flange discontinuities, gaps, connection tabs and other locations where the anchor could disengage.
* Do not allow sliding beam anchors to travel beyond the usable beam flange.
* Do not rely on connection tabs, bolts or adjacent structural members as travel stops unless specifically verified as suitable for that purpose.
* Use manufacturer-approved travel stops or another approved method of preventing unintended disengagement where required.
* Maintain continuous fall protection when installing, repositioning or removing anchorage connectors while exposed to a fall hazard.
* Evaluate swing fall exposure and available fall clearance before use.
* Inspect anchorage connectors before each use and remove defective equipment from service.
* Keep hands and fingers clear of clamp jaws and adjustment mechanisms.

Do not recommend improvised travel stops or unauthorized equipment modifications.
4. Regression Scenarios
Include at minimum:

1. Install beamers.
2. Use beamers while erecting structural steel.
3. Inspect sliding beam anchors.
4. Install pivot D-ring sliding beam clamp.
5. Walk along beam using a beamer.
6. Slide beamer toward beam end.
7. Use beamer on beam with connection tabs and an air gap at the end.
8. Transfer between beams using sliding beam anchors.
9. Remove temporary beam anchors.
10. Install lifting beam clamps for rigging.
11. Install fixed fall protection beam clamps.
12. Use sliding beam anchors near flange discontinuities.

Ensure the appropriate hazards and controls are suggested without requiring the foreman to describe the failure mechanism explicitly.
Preserve contextual distinctions between fall protection, rigging and mechanical fastening.
Incorporate these additions into the permanent safety intelligence records and the v1.4.1-dev3 regression suite.
No production release is authorized.
