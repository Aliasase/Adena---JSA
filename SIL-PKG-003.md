# SIL-PKG-003 — DRILL AND MECHANICAL FASTENING

Date: November 26, 2026
Phase: A-4 — Safety Intelligence Library Development
Production baseline: v1.4.0-p1 — 76ac7c7

## 1. Objective
Develop reusable safety intelligence for drilling, boring, mechanical anchoring and powered fastening across construction, industrial maintenance, manufacturing, surface mining and themed entertainment. Preserve production and approved canonical records. Starting repository: 74 active hazards, 272 registered controls, 266 implemented controls, nine confirmed policies, six withheld bolting controls. New control IDs start C-000273 subject to registry confirmation.

## 4. Candidate hazard and control records
Reconcile H-DF-001 through H-DF-040 against existing canonical records before assigning permanent records. Reuse equivalent controls and retain method-specific applicability.

### H-DF-001 — Contact with rotating drill bits and cutting tools
Applicability: drilling, reaming, countersinking, powered thread tapping.
- Keep hands clear of rotating bits, cutters and chucks.
- Do not touch rotating attachments to guide or stabilize them.
- Allow rotation to stop before handling the workpiece.
- Maintain required guards and protective devices.
- Use fixtures/supports rather than holding small workpieces by hand.
Reuse equivalent rotating-tool contact and entanglement records.

### H-DF-002 — Entanglement with rotating drilling equipment
Applicability: handheld drills, drill presses, magnetic drills and other rotating equipment.
- Keep loose clothing, jewelry, hair and equipment attachments clear of rotating components.
- Secure long hair.
- Do not wear gloves where they create an entanglement hazard at exposed rotating machinery.
- Follow machine-specific operating and guarding requirements.
- Stop rotation before clearing accumulated material.
Reconcile H-BT-010. Company glove requirements do not authorize exposure of gloves to rotating machinery; resolve conflicts through a safe work method before proceeding.

### H-DF-003 — Sudden drill movement from binding or breakthrough
Applicability: handheld drills, rotary hammers, hole saws, large-diameter drilling.
- Reuse torque-reaction and kickback controls.
- Use manufacturer-provided auxiliary handle where required.
- Maintain stable stance and secure grip.
- Anticipate sudden movement at binding or breakthrough.
- Reduce feed pressure approaching breakthrough.
- Stop drill before freeing bound bit.
- Use suitable torque-limiting or anti-kickback features where appropriate.
Reconcile H-BT-004 and C-000106.

### H-DF-004 — Drill bit, cutter or attachment failure
Applicability: powered drilling and boring.
- Inspect bits, cutters, chucks and attachments.
- Select attachments suitable for material and method.
- Use correct size and shank configuration.
- Secure attachments per manufacturer instructions.
- Observe specified speed and feed limits.
- Reject bent, cracked, excessively worn or damaged attachments.
- Do not force equipment beyond intended capacity.
Reconcile H-BT-006.

### H-DF-005 — Ejected drill chuck key or unsecured attachment
Applicability: keyed chucks and applicable drilling machines.
- Remove chuck key before energizing.
- Verify secure bit/cutter installation.
- Do not leave adjustment tools attached to rotating equipment.
- Use approved retention components.
Do not apply chuck-key controls to keyless systems.

### H-DF-006 — Flying chips, fragments and drilling debris
Applicability: drilling, reaming, anchor installation, mechanical fastening.
- Wear safety glasses.
- Use supplemental face protection when fragments may strike face.
- Use goggles where particles may bypass glasses.
- Direct chips away from personnel and protect nearby workers.
- Remove chips with appropriate brush/tool after rotation stops.
Reconcile H-HW-029 and existing eye protection.

### H-DF-007 — Cuts and punctures from sharp bits, swarf and drilled edges
Applicability: metal drilling, sheet metal fastening, handling cuttings.
- Keep hands clear of sharp edges and burrs.
- Remove chips using suitable tools; never clear sharp swarf with bare hands.
- Deburr where procedure permits.
- Wear appropriate hand protection handling stationary sharp materials.
- Allow hot chips and cutters to cool.
Reuse H-BT-002 and thermal-contact controls.

### H-DF-008 — Uncontrolled rotation or movement of workpiece
Applicability: drill presses, handheld drilling, unsecured material.
- Secure workpiece with clamps, fixtures or vise.
- Do not hold small workpieces by hand during machine drilling.
- Position supports to prevent rotation/displacement.
- Keep hands clear of movement paths.
- Stop machine if workpiece shifts.
Reconcile H-HW-031.

### H-DF-009 — Unexpected startup during attachment changes or servicing
Applicability: powered drilling and fastening.
- Disconnect power or remove battery before attachment changes where required.
- Isolate pneumatic energy before servicing.
- Unload powder-actuated tools before cleaning or servicing.
- Isolate fuel and stored energy per manufacturer instructions.
- Prevent unintended activation during jam clearing or adjustments.
Reuse unexpected-startup controls.

### H-DF-010 — Loss of magnetic drill holding force
Applicability: magnetic-base drilling.
- Reuse C-000107.
- Verify mounting surface suitability and remove material preventing full contact.
- Confirm adequate holding force before drilling.
- Install manufacturer-required safety chain or secondary restraint.
- Secure secondary attachment to suitable independent point.
- Do not operate where thickness, geometry or surface prevents adequate holding force.
- Stop if base shifts or loses holding force.
- Account for holding-force loss during power interruption.
Retain distinct hazard where specialized controls required.

### H-DF-011 — Movement or detachment of core drilling equipment
Applicability: stand-mounted and rig-mounted core drills.
- Secure stand by manufacturer-approved mounting/anchoring method.
- Verify supporting surface suitability.
- Inspect stand connections and mounting components.
- Do not rely on unsuitable or damaged surface.
- Use vacuum mounting only in permitted configurations.
- Provide required secondary restraint for applicable orientations.
- Stop if stand moves or loses attachment.
No universal mounting method.

### H-DF-012 — Falling drill cores and breakthrough material
Applicability: through-drilling walls, elevated slabs, floors, overhead structures.
- Determine emergence location of bit and core.
- Establish opposite-side exclusion zone.
- Prevent entry to expected core drop area.
- Provide suitable core retention/retrieval.
- Support heavy cores before separation.
- Coordinate with opposite-side personnel.
- Guard or cover resulting openings as required.
Reuse dropped-object and severed-material controls.

### H-DF-013 — Contact with concealed energized electrical systems
Applicability: drilling/fastening walls, slabs, floors, ceilings, equipment.
- Identify concealed electrical systems; review drawings and utility information.
- Use suitable locating/scanning where concealed services may exist.
- Verify acceptable drilling location and depth.
- Isolate electrical systems where required.
- Stop at unidentified obstruction or suspected electrical service.
- Do not rely solely on assumptions about concealed utility locations.
Distinct from tool supply shock.

### H-DF-014 — Release from concealed piping or process systems
Applicability: drilling/fastening near embedded piping, process equipment, utilities.
- Identify piping and contents; review drawings and utility information.
- Verify drilling location and depth.
- Isolate affected systems where required.
- Stop at unidentified piping or unexpected fluid release.
- Follow emergency procedure if hazardous service damaged.
Reuse process-release controls.

### H-DF-015 — Sudden release of stored energy from post-tensioning tendons
Applicability: drilling, cutting or fastening potentially post-tensioned concrete.
- Determine whether post-tensioning is present.
- Obtain required authorization before drilling/cutting.
- Locate tendons by approved methods.
- Establish approved locations and depth limits.
- Do not drill into or damage tendons.
- Stop when tendon locations cannot reliably be established.
- Restrict access and notify responsible personnel if tendon damaged.
Separate from ordinary reinforcement contact.

### H-DF-016 — Structural weakening from unauthorized drilling or reinforcement damage
Applicability: load-bearing steel, reinforced/prestressed concrete, structural connections.
- Verify authorized locations and hole sizes.
- Follow approved drawings and structural requirements.
- Do not cut reinforcement or enlarge structural holes without authorization.
- Obtain approval for drilling outside approved design.
- Stop at unexpected reinforcement or structural components.
- Report unauthorized/misplaced holes for evaluation.
Reconcile H-BT-013.

### H-DF-017 — Respirable crystalline silica exposure
Applicability: concrete, masonry, stone and other silica-containing materials.
- Identify silica-containing materials.
- Use applicable approved silica exposure-control method.
- Use integrated water delivery where specified for task/equipment.
- Use suitable dust extraction/collection where specified.
- Maintain shrouds, hoses, filters and collectors.
- Use HEPA-filtered vacuum for hole cleaning where required by applicable method.
- Avoid dry sweeping and uncontrolled compressed-air cleaning.
- Follow applicable respiratory protection requirements and maintain required exposure-control documentation.
No universal N95; wet drilling does not necessarily eliminate respirator requirements. Distinguish handheld drilling, rig-mounted core drilling and other equipment categories in regulatory validation.

### H-DF-018 — Inhalation of non-silica drilling dust
Applicability: wood, drywall, metals, composites and other dust-producing materials.
- Identify material and dust hazards.
- Use effective source capture.
- Prevent unnecessary airborne dispersion.
- Use suitable cleaning methods.
- Evaluate exposure and respiratory protection.
- Follow material-specific safety information.
Do not classify all dust as silica.

### H-DF-019 — Exposure to hazardous coatings or existing building materials
Applicability: painted, coated or potentially hazardous existing materials.
- Identify coatings/materials before disturbance; review surveys.
- Follow applicable lead, asbestos or other hazardous-material procedures.
- Do not disturb unidentified suspect materials until evaluated.
- Use approved exposure controls for confirmed hazards.
Reuse existing records. Age or paint alone does not establish lead/asbestos.

### H-DF-020 — Electrical shock during wet drilling
Applicability: electric drilling with water or conductive surroundings.
- Use equipment suitable for wet application.
- Provide required ground-fault protection.
- Protect connections from water; inspect cords/plugs/components.
- Prevent water accumulation around electrical connections.
- Stop if water enters equipment not designed for exposure.
- Follow manufacturer electrical protection requirements.
Reuse H-BT-026.

### H-DF-021 — Slips from drilling water and slurry
Applicability: wet drilling, core drilling, slurry operations.
- Contain water/slurry; prevent spread into access routes.
- Remove slurry and standing water promptly.
- Maintain safe walking/working surfaces.
- Protect adjacent equipment and areas from runoff.
- Collect/dispose per project environmental requirements.
Reconcile slip/housekeeping hazards.

### H-DF-022 — Chemical irritation or burns from wet cement slurry
Applicability: cementitious materials with alkaline slurry exposure.
- Avoid prolonged skin contact.
- Wear suitable chemical-resistant gloves and clothing where required.
- Protect eyes from splash.
- Remove contaminated clothing promptly.
- Wash affected skin with clean water.
- Follow SDS and medical-response requirements.
Do not assume identical chemistry for all drilling fluids.

### H-DF-023 — Skin or respiratory exposure to anchor adhesives
Applicability: adhesive anchors and chemical fastening.
- Review SDS.
- Wear compatible gloves.
- Provide appropriate ventilation.
- Avoid skin/eye contact; use suitable eye/face protection for splash.
- Clean spills by approved methods.
- Follow manufacturer handling, storage and disposal instructions.
- Follow exposure-based respiratory protection requirements.

### H-DF-024 — Mechanical anchor failure or pullout
Applicability: expansion anchors, concrete screws, sleeve anchors and other mechanical anchors.
- Verify specified anchor type, size, application and substrate suitability.
- Follow approved installation instructions.
- Use specified hole diameter/depth, edge distances and spacing.
- Clean holes per applicable installation procedure.
- Use specified setting tools and installation torque.
- Complete required inspection/testing before loading.
- No unauthorized substitutions.
Distinguish equipment, structural, lifting and fall protection anchors.

### H-DF-025 — Adhesive anchor failure from incorrect installation or curing
Applicability: adhesive anchors and bonded threaded rods.
- Verify approved system, expiration and storage conditions.
- Confirm substrate and orientation permitted.
- Follow specified drilling and hole-cleaning procedures.
- Use correct dispensing/mixing components and initial dispensing procedure.
- Install to specified embedment.
- Observe required temperature and curing conditions.
- Do not disturb/load before required cure complete.
- Complete required inspection and acceptance before loading.
No universal cleaning sequence, cure time or temperature.

### H-DF-026 — Failure of anchors used for life safety or lifting
Applicability: fall protection, suspended access, lifting and life-safety systems.
- Use anchors specifically approved for intended application.
- Verify supporting structure and anchorage design.
- Follow approved installation/inspection requirements.
- Obtain required qualified-person or engineering approval.
- Complete required testing and acceptance before use.
- Do not use ordinary construction anchors for life safety/lifting without approval.
No universal anchorage capacity.

### H-DF-027 — Unintended discharge of powder-actuated fastening tool
Applicability: powder-actuated fastening.
- Only personnel trained for specific tool may operate.
- Inspect tool and safety devices; perform prescribed functional checks.
- Load only when preparing to fasten.
- Point safely, never at another person.
- Keep fingers off trigger until ready.
- Do not bypass contact/firing safety mechanisms.
- Never leave loaded tool unattended.
- Unload before servicing/storage.

### H-DF-028 — Fastener ricochet, fragmentation or rebound
Applicability: powder-actuated, gas-actuated and other applicable high-energy fastening.
- Verify substrate suitability.
- Use approved fasteners and compatible equipment.
- Select appropriate driving energy per manufacturer.
- Maintain required edge distances/spacing.
- Avoid unsuitable, excessively hard or brittle substrates.
- Avoid existing holes/damaged locations unless specifically permitted.
- Keep personnel clear of ricochet/fragment path.
- Wear required primary eye and supplemental face protection.
Do not universalize substrate restrictions or driving energy settings.

### H-DF-029 — Fastener penetration through substrate
Applicability: PAT, powered nailing, stapling and penetrating fastening.
- Verify substrate thickness and fastener length.
- Determine personnel/equipment behind surface; protect opposite side.
- Keep hands/body clear of expected path.
- Do not fasten toward concealed services or occupied areas without verification/protection.
- Use method specified for substrate.

### H-DF-030 — Delayed discharge following PAT misfire
Applicability: powder-actuated tools.
- Maintain prescribed safe position and direction after misfire.
- Follow manufacturer-specific misfire waiting and unloading procedure.
- Do not immediately open/disassemble misfired tool.
- Handle misfired loads by approved procedure.
- Remove defective tools from service.
No universal waiting period without verification.

### H-DF-031 — Fire or explosion from powder loads or gas-actuated fastening
Applicability: powder-actuated and fuel-gas fastening equipment.
- Do not use in explosive/flammable atmospheres where prohibited.
- Store loads/fuel cells per manufacturer; protect from excessive heat/ignition.
- Use compatible loads/fuel cells only.
- Remove damaged/leaking fuel containers from service.
- Follow approved disposal procedures.
Do not classify every PAT operation as hot work absent ignition exposure or applicable project requirement.

### H-DF-032 — Unintended nail or staple discharge
Applicability: pneumatic, electric, battery and gas nailers/staplers.
- Inspect trigger and work-contact safety mechanisms.
- Keep fingers off trigger when moving/repositioning.
- Point away from personnel.
- Do not bypass work-contact safety mechanisms.
- Use appropriate manufacturer-approved actuation mode.
- Isolate power before jam clearing/servicing.
- Do not carry by trigger.
- Prevent discharge when placing/retrieving tool.

### H-DF-033 — Nail or staple deflection into hands or body
Applicability: powered nailing/stapling.
- Keep hands clear of anticipated path.
- Use clamps or suitable holding means for small materials.
- Account for knots, existing fasteners and hard inclusions.
- Do not position hands behind fastening surface.
- Maintain stable tool position.
- Select suitable fastener length and driving settings.

### H-DF-034 — Ejected rivet mandrels and fastening components
Applicability: blind riveting and applicable fastening.
- Wear safety glasses and supplemental face protection where required.
- Use correct nosepiece and fastener.
- Maintain mandrel collection/retention devices.
- Keep personnel clear of ejection path.
- Collect spent mandrels promptly.
- Remove damaged/malfunctioning equipment from service.
Reconcile ejected-component hazards.

### H-DF-035 — Pinch and crush injuries during anchor setting or riveting
Applicability: anchor setting, riveting, powered fastening.
- Keep fingers clear of setting tools and mating surfaces.
- Use suitable holding/positioning tools.
- Keep hands clear of moving jaws and powered mechanisms.
- Secure materials before fastening.
- Coordinate hand placement with others.
- Isolate equipment before clearing obstructions.
Reuse equivalent pinch/crush hazards.

### H-DF-036 — Impact injuries from hammer-driven anchor setting
Applicability: manual anchor setting, pin driving, hammer-assisted fastening.
- Use specified setting tool.
- Inspect striking tools; remove mushroomed/damaged tools.
- Use suitable holding devices where practical.
- Keep hands clear of strike path.
- Wear appropriate eye protection.
- Maintain stable working position.
Reuse H-BT-029 and H-BT-030.

### H-DF-037 — Musculoskeletal strain from sustained drilling force
Applicability: large-diameter, overhead, repetitive drilling and heavy fastening equipment.
- Select equipment appropriate to required force.
- Use mechanical supports/stands where practical.
- Reduce overhead reaching and awkward posture.
- Maintain stable footing and positioning.
- Avoid excessive force.
- Rotate repetitive/demanding tasks where appropriate.
- Use suitable handling assistance for heavy equipment.
Reconcile H-BT-007/008.

### H-DF-038 — Hand-arm vibration from percussion drilling and fastening
Applicability: hammer drills, rotary hammers, impact drivers and applicable fastening tools.
- Select suitable equipment; maintain tools and attachments.
- Avoid excessive grip force.
- Manage exposure duration according to task and assessment.
- Rotate tasks where appropriate.
- Report persistent numbness, tingling or loss of sensation.
- Do not rely solely on anti-vibration gloves.
Reuse H-BT-020.

### H-DF-039 — Battery damage, overheating or thermal runaway
Applicability: battery-powered drilling/fastening.
- Inspect for damage, swelling or abnormal heating.
- Use compatible manufacturer-approved batteries/chargers.
- Protect from impact, moisture and excessive heat.
- Prevent terminals contacting conductive objects.
- Remove damaged/malfunctioning batteries from service.
- Follow approved emergency procedures for smoking, overheating or burning batteries.
- Do not charge damaged packs.
Reusable for future battery equipment.

### H-DF-040 — Compressed-air injection or hose-related injury
Applicability: pneumatic drills, nailers, staplers, riveters.
- Use rated hoses/connections; secure with applicable positive retention.
- Inspect hoses/couplings.
- Isolate and relieve pressure before servicing.
- Do not direct compressed air toward body.
- Follow applicable restraint and excess-flow requirements.
- Observe manufacturer operating pressure limits.
Reuse H-BT-025 and pneumatic controls; separate injection from hose whip where distinct.

## 7. Recognition collision prevention
- Drill alone does not establish concrete or silica.
- Core drilling does not establish oilfield drilling.
- Drill press does not establish pressurized process equipment.
- Impact driver does not automatically establish impact wrench.
- Magnetic drill does not establish magnetic lifting.
- Anchor alone does not establish drilling/installation; inspection of existing fall anchor does not establish installation.
- Wedge anchor does not establish wedge rigging equipment.
- Powder coating does not establish PAT.
- Gas-actuated fastening does not establish oxy-fuel cutting.
- Brad nailer does not establish PAT; nail gun alone does not establish pneumatic operation.
- PAT requires fastening context where ambiguous.
- SDS distinguishes safety data sheet from drill attachment.
- Tap distinguishes thread tapping from hot tapping and water taps.
- Post-tensioned concrete establishes tendon exposure only when work may affect system.
- Adhesive use alone does not establish adhesive anchor installation.
- Stainless steel alone does not establish stainless lath.
- Mechanical fastening does not automatically establish hot work.
- Concrete and steel drilling may coexist in same task.
